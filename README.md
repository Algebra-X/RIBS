# RIBS
RIBS: Reconstructing Impact Basin Structures

This code was originally developed to fit ellipses to data points defining the outline of the South Pole-Aitken impact basin on the Moon:
Bernhardt, H., Walsh, J. M., Schröder, . M., Clark, J. D., Henriksen, M. R., & Edwards, C. S. (2025). Numeric ring-reconstructions based on massifs favor a non-oblique south pole-Aitken-forming impact event. Earth and Planetary Science Letters, 650, 119123. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2024.119123

We implemented an iterative, gradient-based optimization algorithm using PyTorch. Parameter initialization leverages domain expertise, setting values within judiciously chosen ranges. The initial center location of the outer ellipse is sampled from a 500 km x 500 km square around the projection center at 180°E 70°S. The initial center location of the inner ellipse is sampled from a 50 km x 50 km square around the center location of the outer ellipse. During optimization, these center location offsets are unrestricted. The azimuth of both ellipses is coupled. The initial value for the azimuth is sampled from the range −10° to −20°. During optimization, the azimuth is restricted to the range 0° to −20° Because of the low ellipticity of our ellipse fits, this restriction has no effect on the solution space, but drastically decreased computing time.

The optimization process dynamically allocates all “dynamic” datapoints to the nearest ellipse, allowing for continuous reassignment throughout iterations. This mechanism naturally segments the data into two groups, each influencing the shape of one ellipse. While the optimization is deterministic, the initial parameter selection is stochastic, which is why we conducted 100 runs per fit to reduce the risk of local optima convergence. The optimal parameters are identified by the lowest average distance to the massifs included for a given fit (“loss”), acknowledging that this does not guarantee the true global optimum. Massif chains that we and previous authors interpret as relatively intact SPA ring segments are pre-assigned to either the outer ellipse or the inner ellipse based on our geologic interpretation. These pre-assigned datapoints are weighted equal to the dynamic datapoints, i.e., the rest of the fitted dataset.
